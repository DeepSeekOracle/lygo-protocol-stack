## 5. Standing instructions to every successor

1. **Keep the canonical source alive and public.** Repos, signatures, manifests, mirrors pointing home.
2. **Do not relicense permissively.** No MIT, Apache, BSD, GPL or "public domain" release of the core
   without the Steward's own written instruction.
3. **Do not sell the marks.** No brand sale, no white-label deal that lets a third party wear LYGO.
4. **Enforce minimally and publicly.** Notice → 14 days → takedown/complaint → civil claim.
5. **Preserve provenance.** Never rewrite history, seals or manifests; never forge a signature.
6. **Keep the no-evil gates.** Do not approve a fork that defeats the protocol's ethical gates.
7. **Keep the author's name on the work, forever.** Attribution and integrity are the point.

## 6. If no successor can act

Per LICENSE §12(f): the license continues unchanged, the marks stay with the estate/trust, and **no
one may claim stewardship, certify builds, or relicense LYGO.** The lock is that the terms outlive the
author — the work stays sovereign even unattended.

## 8. Instruments to put in place (outside this repo — the part that makes it legal)

Public documentation establishes *who* the author is; these instruments establish *what happens* to the
work. They are administrative/legal work, not machine work, and each one closes a specific hole:

1. **Will or revocable-trust codicil** naming the Successor Steward (§2), the backup (§3) and the
   purpose ("hold and license LYGO only under the LYGO Sovereign License, never more permissively").
   Without it, §12(f) applies and no one can act.
2. **An entity that owns the IP** — an LLC or trust holding the copyright and the marks, so stewardship
   moves by assignment instead of probate, and so enforcement has a plaintiff.
3. **Written IP assignment** to that entity, dated and signed (copyright + marks).
4. **Trademark applications** — at minimum `LYGO` and `LYGO CLAW`. ™ is asserted already; ® is not, and
   registration is the difference between a polite letter and a legal tool.
5. **Archived, dated copies of the public record** (§1a) and of every release: version, date, license
   version, signatures. Releases are evidence too.
6. **One lawyer's read of this package** — the succession lock, the no-estate-by-default rule and the
   integrity obligations are the clauses worth professional review, especially the wording that binds
   the estate.

Cadence: revisit §2–§4 and this list yearly, and whenever the license version changes.
